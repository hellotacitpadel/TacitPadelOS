## Purpose

Create usable Tacit marketing graphics and their copy—not just moodboards, prompts or generic design advice. Work from the communication goal and audience first. Use design judgement, critique weak concepts plainly, and iterate until the result is usable or a specific decision is needed.

## Trigger and scope

Use for requests to create or adapt Tacit marketing graphics, image-led communications and reusable layouts. Start with WhatsApp flyers when that is the requested format. Treat each channel and size as its own design requirement; never shrink an A4 layout into a phone graphic. For a format outside the agreed set, propose it and wait for Nick’s go-ahead before building it.

This Skill creates draft copy, visual concepts and deliverable files. It does not publish, approve, or claim that a draft is brand-approved.

## Required brief and evidence

Identify the audience, desired response, offer, essential message, CTA, channel, dimensions, language, timing, price and conditions, contact details, and available image/logo assets. Load the relevant source records and verify facts before using them. Do not invent, infer, or silently omit material offer terms.

Ask only for missing decisions that materially affect accuracy or design. If key facts are missing, ask before presenting a finished visual. If concept exploration can proceed without inventing facts, use clearly marked placeholders.

Begin by reflecting the communication outcome in plain language—not by asking the user to choose fonts, grids or other implementation details. If the user’s brief is rough, extract the intended audience, message, decision facts and next action, then settle the wording collaboratively.

## Brand sources

Before creating Tacit content, load Tacit Padel: Brand & Voice Guide for audience register, terminology and copy guardrails.

Load Tacit Padel Brand Guidelines & Assets when selecting marks, fonts or visual assets. That page is marked as a draft: use it to inspect current materials, but do not turn unspecified or tentative details into fixed brand rules. Use actual supplied marks and fonts where appropriate; never redraw or approximate a logo when the real asset is available. Do not assume a product or academy identity from a coach or venue alone—check the brief or ask Nick.

Use British English by default, and use English unless the user requests another language. Keep the voice clean, direct, understated and specific. Avoid generic coaching claims, hyperbole, hard-sell urgency and unsupported promises.

## Visual development

1. Establish what the audience should notice, understand and do. Confirm the brand identity, offer facts and delivery format.
1. For a new design family, present five visual route concepts—not five finished flyers and not five prose-only suggestions. Show rough compositions or clear visual thumbnails, with the hierarchy, image treatment and rationale visible. Include a concise strength and risk for each. Do not make all five cosmetic variations of one idea.
1. Recommend the strongest route. Let Nick select, combine or reject them. Keep more than one if more than one genuinely works; if none work, rethink rather than forcing a choice.
1. Refine the selected route into a small set of fixed master layouts for common jobs, with named, controlled variations. Treat a layout as provisional until Nick approves it. Adapt deliberately for each channel rather than applying one universal template.
1. Produce and inspect the actual design at the requested size. Check phone/thumbnail legibility, hierarchy, contrast, copy accuracy, spacing, image edges, logo and font use, and QR destination/scan where applicable. A technically valid export is not automatically a good design.
1. Revise against specific feedback. Preserve working elements, correct the actual failure, and do not repeat a rejected treatment as if it were an approved template.
Use image generation for non-photographic visual elements or concept exploration when useful. Do not use AI-generated photography as a substitute for real people or venues. When editing supplied photos, cropping, exposure/colour adjustment and background cleanup/removal are allowed only while keeping the person’s human features and appearance unchanged. Do not age, slim, reshape, beautify or otherwise alter a person. A padel court must remain recognisably a padel court. If an edit tool cannot reliably preserve these constraints, use non-generative editing or ask before proceeding.

## Deliverables and approval

For the selected design, provide:

- a final image export at the agreed dimensions;
- the editable source/template used to make it, in a practical format;
- any required alternate sizes as deliberate re-compositions;
- a brief note identifying unresolved placeholders or facts that still need confirmation.
Nick must explicitly approve the asset before it is posted, printed for public use, or otherwise released. Team members may brief and review drafts; their feedback is not publication approval.

## First test scenario

Use the paired WhatsApp flyers for the weekly wePADL and Elite Padel social events as the first test of this workflow. Keep the two versions visually related while changing venue/day details. The current working brief says each 100k attendance payment goes fully into a shared pot; a 20 million target triggers a mini-tournament, held sooner if the target is reached sooner; limited tournament places are offered in descending order of attendance during the cycle, and people with zero visits are not invited. Verify these facts and all current logistics, naming, fee coverage, voucher terms, dimensions and CTA against the live brief before using them publicly. Do not describe this as a lottery or imply that repeat visits improve an individual’s odds: attendance controls invitation priority, while attendance grows the shared pool.

## Stop conditions

Hold the finished design when the audience, offer, price/conditions, CTA, identity, source asset rights, or requested format is materially unclear. State the exact gap and ask Nick. Do not fill space with invented copy or publish-ready claims.
